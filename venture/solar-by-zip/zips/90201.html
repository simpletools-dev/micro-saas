Solar potential in Bell Gardens (90201)

Bell Gardens gets about 6.12 kWh/m²/day of sunshine on average over the year (NREL data for Los Angeles). At California's current average residential rate of 33.61¢/kWh (EIA, July 2026), a 3.5 kW rooftop system here could generate roughly 6,260 kWh per year — about enough to cover a typical California home's electricity use.

Over 25 years, that production is worth an estimated $39,000 to $72,000 in avoided electricity purchases, depending on how fast grid rates rise. That range is before the cost of the system and before incentives like the federal solar tax credit — see the assumptions box for exactly what's included.

How we estimate — assumptions

  • Home use: 6,500 kWh/year (approx. average California household, EIA RECS 2020). Your use may differ a lot — check your utility bill.
  • System size: sized to cover ~100% of that use: 6,500 ÷ (6.12 × 365 × 0.80), rounded to the nearest 0.5 kW (kept between 3 and 15 kW).
  • Production: 6.12 kWh/m²/day × 365 days × 0.80 performance ratio per kW of panels (accounts for inverter, heat, wiring and soiling losses).
  • Sunshine source: NREL PVWatts v8, NSRDB typical-year weather data, measured at the Los Angeles reference point (data as of 2026-09-28).
  • Electricity rate: 33.61¢/kWh — EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A, California residential average, July 2026. Your utility's rate plan and season will differ.
  • Net metering: year-1 savings assume 85% of production displaces full-retail purchases. California's NEM 3.0 pays below-retail rates for exported surplus, so we don't assume 100%.
  • 25-year range: low = rates stay flat and only 75% of production offsets retail purchases; high = rates rise 2.5%/year and 100% offsets. Before system cost, financing, maintenance and incentives.
  • Not modeled: roof direction and tilt, shading from trees/buildings, panel degradation (~0.5%/yr typical), or local permit/utility fees.
