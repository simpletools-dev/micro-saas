Federal SI policy timeline

Every event below comes from public reporting, with sources linked. Summaries are neutral and factual. Each entry adds a short "why it matters" note: that is analysis built from the cited facts, clearly separated from the reporting.

Note on dates: the Super Intelligence Force was given 120 days to report starting around October 4, 2026, which implies a report around early February 2027. That date is approximate, not confirmed.
October 15, 2026

SIF holds its first meeting

The Super Intelligence Force was scheduled to hold its first meeting on October 15, 2026, with leadership expected to publish goals and responsibilities afterward.

The first meeting was scheduled for Thursday, October 15, 2026, at 4 p.m. Reporting based on the White House schedule listed a long invitation roster beyond the four leaders: Vice President JD Vance, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, Deputy Chief of Staff Richard Walters, OSTP Director Michael Kratsios, and National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross. Leadership said goals and responsibilities would be published after the meeting.

Why it matters

Why it matters: the first meeting started the clock in earnest. The invitation list shows how many parts of government want a voice in AI policy, from Treasury to Commerce to the intelligence community. The published goals and responsibilities are the next document to watch.

Sources: Govt Schemes

October 6, 2026

Justice Department adopts SI terminology in staff memo

Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter issued a Justice Department memo directing staff to use 'super intelligence' and 'SI' instead of 'AI' in official communications and, where appropriate, court filings, implementing the September executive order.

Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter issued the memo on October 6. It requires "super intelligence" and "SI" instead of AI "whenever federal law permits," across public statements, policy documents, records, and other communications, and says the change can extend to court filings when appropriate. Existing regulations, contracts, grants, and historical documents do not have to be rewritten; the new language applies mainly to future materials.

Why it matters

Why it matters: the Justice Department is one of Washington's largest legal institutions, and its directive gives the rename its first real test inside enforcement. Wording that appears in court filings starts to enter the legal record itself.

Sources: Analytics Insight

October 4, 2026

Super Intelligence Force (SIF) announced

President Trump announced the Super Intelligence Force (SIF) in a Truth Social post, a federal task force chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton with three vice chairs, given 120 days to report on AI risks and opportunities and the federal government's role, implying a report around early February 2027.

The leadership mix is revealing. Chair Jay Clayton became Director of National Intelligence in August 2026 after serving as US attorney for the Southern District of New York and chairing the SEC during the first Trump term. The three vice chairs are FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael, and OPM Director Scott Kupor. Bloomberg reported Clayton would tour Silicon Valley AI firms for meetings with Nvidia's Jensen Huang, OpenAI's Sam Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg. The task force has no reported budget or statutory authority of its own; its members keep their existing jobs. Counting 120 days from the October 4 announcement lands around February 1, 2027; counting from the September 29 executive order lands around January 27, 2027, so the deadline is approximate either way.

Why it matters

Why it matters: the SIF is the administration's main vehicle for AI policy decisions, and its membership shows the priorities: national security first, consumer protection second, military research third, and the federal workforce fourth. A task force with no budget of its own works through the agencies its members run, so watch what those agencies do.

Sources: PYMNTS, Oossa

October 4, 2026

Musk says SpaceXAI will be renamed SpaceXSI

Elon Musk confirmed that SpaceX's AI arm, SpaceXAI, will be renamed SpaceXSI, following the September executive order replacing 'AI' with 'SI' in executive branch usage.

Musk gave no date for the change, and reporting from those days said the unit's official accounts and domains still read SpaceXAI. The rename would not be the unit's first: after SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026, it became a division called SpaceXAI, then was rebranded SpaceXAI in July 2026.

Why it matters

Why it matters: this was the first signal that a major AI company would adopt the federal vocabulary outright, aligning a corporate brand with the executive order's terminology.

Sources: The Hindu BusinessLine (ANI), Lior AI

October 2, 2026

.si domain surge reported: 44,000 September registrations

Reporting from Register.si, Slovenia's domain registry, showed 44,000 new .si registrations in September 2026 versus under 2,000 in August, an increase of more than 2,100%, which the registry called unprecedented.

Register.si, Slovenia's domain registry, called the September figure unprecedented. A second tracker citing registry figures put September at 46,066 registrations versus 3,500 in August, an increase of over 1,100%, while the BBC's reporting cited 44,000 versus under 2,000, an increase of more than 2,100%.

Why it matters

Why it matters: domains are a fast public-vote mechanism. Tens of thousands of people paid money to claim .si addresses within weeks of the rename, which tells you how far the vocabulary traveled beyond Washington in under a month.

Sources: BBC via WeSearch, BizToc

September 29, 2026

White House Accord on Super Intelligence signed

Chief executives of Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, a voluntary industry commitment on AI safety controls and incident reporting.

The chief executives signed the pledge at the White House alongside the president. Reporting noted there was no explicit agreement to rebrand company products, and analysis of the accord stressed that it creates no legally enforceable obligations. As of early October 2026, OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic still described their products as AI.

Why it matters

Why it matters: the accord is a cooperation signal, not a regulation. The industry leaders stood with the administration and accepted voluntary safety commitments while keeping their own product language. Symbolism and substance moved at different speeds.

Sources: Tech Times, Lior AI

September 29, 2026

Executive Order 14434 signed: 'Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence'

President Trump signed Executive Order 14434, 'Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence,' directing executive branch agencies to use 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' in place of 'Artificial Intelligence' and 'AI' in official non-statutory communications, and tasking the science adviser with proposing a formal federal definition.

The order's key mechanics: Section 3(a) defines Super Intelligence as the technologies already covered by the statutory definition of artificial intelligence at 15 U.S.C. 9401(3), so nothing new counts and nothing old stops counting. Section 3(b) gives the White House science adviser 60 days, a deadline analysts date to November 28, 2026, to propose legislative language for a formal SI definition. Section 2(b) says agencies need not rewrite previously issued regulations, contracts, or grants; the new wording applies to future documents. Section 4 states the order creates no rights enforceable in court. Not everyone followed: California's governor reportedly told state agencies to keep saying AI, and a YouGov poll found 53% of Americans preferred "artificial intelligence" against 9% for "super intelligence." The White House itself had not finished the switch: the AI.gov homepage showed "SI.gov" in large type while the address remained AI.gov.

Why it matters

Why it matters: this is the operative document of the rename, and its restraint is the story. It changes government vocabulary, defines nothing new, binds only the executive branch, and leaves laws, contracts, and company product names untouched. The technology did not change; the paperwork did.

Sources: Federal Register, The Register, Lior AI

September 22, 2026

Trump floats AI rename at UN General Assembly

Speaking at the UN General Assembly, President Trump said he intended to rename artificial intelligence, arguing that the word 'artificial' makes the technology sound fake and that US government documents would use 'super intelligence' instead.

That same week, a State Department bureau moved first: Michael Drager, a deputy assistant secretary in the Bureau of International Organization Affairs, emailed staff under the subject line "Change in Nomenclature AI to SI," instructing the bureau to use SI in all remarks, positions, and press material. Because that bureau oversees US missions to the UN and other multilateral bodies, the instruction reached American diplomacy worldwide.

Why it matters

Why it matters: the speech was a signal of intent, but the State Department memo shows the rename was already being implemented inside government days before any executive order. Public announcement came first; paperwork followed.

Sources: Lior AI (citing AP)

Questions

Timeline FAQ

Answers grounded in the sourced events above.

Is the AI to SI rename a law?

No. Executive Order 14434 is a presidential directive to the executive branch. It does not change statutes passed by Congress, existing regulations, contracts, or grants. Replacing the statutory definition of AI would require an Act of Congress. The order itself says it creates no rights enforceable in court.

When is the Super Intelligence Force report due?

The task force was given 120 days. Counting from the October 4, 2026 announcement, that lands around February 1, 2027; counting from the September 29 executive order, around January 27, 2027. Public reporting does not settle which date the clock started on, so treat the deadline as approximate. This site's newsletter sends one email when the report drops.

What did the White House Accord on Super Intelligence actually require?

It is a voluntary industry commitment on AI safety controls and incident reporting, signed by the chief executives of Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia. Analysis of the document stresses it creates no legally enforceable obligations, and there was no explicit agreement for the companies to rebrand their products.

Do private companies have to say SI?

No. The executive order binds departments and agencies of the federal executive branch. It does not bind Congress, the courts, US states, universities, newsrooms, or private companies. As of early October 2026, the major labs still described their products as AI. Elon Musk said SpaceXAI would become SpaceXSI, without giving a date.

What happens on November 28, 2026?

That is the date analysts give for the White House science adviser's deadline to send the president proposed legislative language for a formal federal definition of SI, 60 days after the executive order. It is a proposal, not a law. Until Congress acts, SI means what AI already meant under 15 U.S.C. 9401(3).

Is the government's SI the same as superintelligence in AI research?

No. In research, superintelligence describes a hypothetical system that greatly exceeds human cognitive performance in virtually every domain, the idea popularized by Nick Bostrom's 2014 book Superintelligence. In the executive order, SI is a new label for everything already covered by the existing legal definition of AI. Same word, different meaning, so always check who wrote it and in what context.
