About American-Made Superintelligence

American-Made Superintelligence ("Superintelligence, made in America") is an independent tracker of the US superintelligence race: American AI labs, US chip fabs, data center buildouts, SI-economy jobs, and the federal SI policy timeline. This page documents how the site is built and where it can be wrong.

Methodology

  • One dataset, sourced throughout. All factual claims come from public reporting compiled on October 16, 2026, with the source linked next to each claim. The full source list is below: 46 unique links.
  • Announced is not completed. Investment figures and capacities are announced or reported amounts, not audited totals. Project statuses distinguish operating, under construction, in development, and announced, and the text says which is which.
  • Approximate dates are labeled. The Super Intelligence Force report date (around early February 2027) and the November 28, 2026 definition deadline are marked approximate wherever they appear.
  • Careers links are verified live. The four official careers pages were checked on October 16, 2026. Company pages change; we re-verify on each data refresh.
  • Analysis is labeled. "Why it matters" notes are clearly separated from reporting and built only from the cited facts. They introduce no new factual claims.
  • No invented material. No invented facts, figures, URLs, testimonials, or affiliate links. Affiliate and ad slots are placeholders, clearly labeled, with href="#" until real programs are approved.

Per-section sourcing

  • Policy timeline: 8 events from September 22 to October 15, 2026. Each event links its reporting. "Why it matters" notes are analysis built from the cited facts, clearly separated from the reporting. The SIF report date is approximate and labeled as such wherever it appears.
  • AI labs: 4 labs with US buildout descriptions, a side-by-side comparison table, and official careers pages verified live on October 16, 2026. Scale figures are announced or reported amounts.
  • Chip fabs: 5 US fabrication and advanced packaging projects. Investment figures are company announcements; statuses are as reported on October 16, 2026. Timelines slip, and the page says which projects are producing versus planned.
  • Data centers: 21 projects across 13 states with a filterable table. Each row links its sources. Project spotlights add verified detail on the four most consequential campuses.
  • Jobs: Role categories and reported employment figures from the tracked projects, not listings. No salary figures are published because we have no verified compensation data. Careers links verified October 16, 2026.
  • Explainers: Plain-English explainers drawn from the sourced timeline and buildout data. Each carries its own source links; the FAQ answers restate sourced facts.

Limitations

  • Data changes fast. Figures here reflect reporting available on October 16, 2026. Timelines slip, scopes change, and some projects stall. The site is refreshed from public reporting; it is not a live feed.
  • We track what is reported. We do not independently verify company announcements or audit investment figures. Where a figure is a company's own claim, the page says so.
  • Coverage is US-only by design. International labs, fabs, and data centers are out of scope, even when they matter to the story.
  • Neutral tone. Summaries are factual and non-editorial. The site takes no position on the policies or projects it tracks.
  • Not affiliated. This site is not affiliated with the US government or any company mentioned.

Reading the numbers

Three kinds of numbers appear on this site, and they mean different things.

  • Announced figures are what a company or government said it will spend or build: the $500 billion Stargate program, the $265 billion TSMC Arizona commitment, Meta's $50B+ Hyperion scope. They describe intent and can change.
  • Reported figures come from journalism about the projects: 7,000 tradespeople at Abilene, 440,000 GB300 GPUs in use at Colossus by late September 2026, 44,000 September .si registrations. They describe what reporters could verify at the time.
  • Operating figures describe what is actually running: TSMC Fab 21 Phase 1 in volume production, the first two Abilene buildings operational, Project Rainier operational. The site labels which kind each number is wherever it appears.

Update cadence

The dataset behind this site was compiled on October 16, 2026, and every page is regenerated from it by a single build script, so the whole site stays consistent. A refresh means re-checking the sources, updating statuses and figures, and rebuilding. Between refreshes, the source links on every claim are the freshness check: open them to see whether the reporting has moved on.

Milestones that would trigger an update include the proposed SI definition due November 28, 2026, the Super Intelligence Force report due around early February 2027, fab production starts, and data center openings.

What this site does not do

  • Not a job board. The jobs page describes role categories and reported project employment; it does not list openings. Careers links go to the companies directly.
  • Not investment advice. Figures describe announced corporate and infrastructure spending. Nothing here is a recommendation about any security or company.
  • Not legal advice. The policy timeline summarizes public reporting on executive actions and agency memos. It does not interpret anyone's legal obligations.
  • Not affiliated. Independent and unaffiliated with the US government and every company mentioned.

How to cite this site

If you reference this tracker in your own work, cite the underlying reporting rather than this page wherever possible: every claim links its source. When you need to cite the tracker itself, a suggested form is:

American-Made Superintelligence, "Page title," americanmade.si, data accessed October 16, 2026, URL.

Because figures change as projects proceed, always include the access date. The per-claim source links are the authoritative references; this site is a finding aid, not a primary source.
