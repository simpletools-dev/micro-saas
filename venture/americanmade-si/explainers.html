Policy

What the AI to SI rename means

On September 29, 2026, President Trump signed Executive Order 14434, titled "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence." The order directs executive branch agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in official non-statutory communications: correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, and policy documents, "to the maximum extent permitted by law." A week earlier, at the UN General Assembly on September 22, the president had said he intended to rename artificial intelligence because "artificial" makes it sound fake.

The mechanics matter more than the headline. Section 3(a) defines Super Intelligence as the technologies already covered by the statutory definition of artificial intelligence at 15 U.S.C. 9401(3): nothing new counts, nothing old stops counting. Section 3(b) gives the White House science adviser 60 days, a deadline analysts date to November 28, 2026, to propose legislative language for a formal SI definition. Section 2(b) says agencies need not rewrite previously issued regulations, contracts, grants, or historical documents. Section 4 states the order creates no rights enforceable in court.

  • Who is bound: departments and agencies of the federal executive branch. Not Congress, not the courts, not states, not universities, not newsrooms, not private companies, not other countries.
  • It is being implemented: a State Department bureau moved within days of the UN speech, and on October 6 the Justice Department issued a staff memo directing employees to use "super intelligence" and "SI" in official communications and, where appropriate, court filings.
  • Industry is following at its own pace: the chief executives of Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia signed a voluntary, non-binding White House Accord on Super Intelligence the same week. There was no agreement to rebrand products, and as of early October 2026 the major labs still described their products as AI. Elon Musk said SpaceXAI would become SpaceXSI, without a date.
  • Not everyone is on board: California's governor reportedly told state agencies to keep saying AI, and a YouGov poll found 53% of Americans preferred "artificial intelligence" against 9% for "super intelligence."
  • The public noticed: .si domain registrations jumped from under 2,000 in August 2026 to 44,000 in September, an increase of more than 2,100% reported by Register.si via the BBC.
  • Two meanings of one word: in research, superintelligence describes a hypothetical system greatly exceeding human cognitive performance in virtually every domain. In the order, SI is a new label for everything already called AI. When a government document says SI, it is not claiming research-grade superintelligence.

Policy

What the Super Intelligence Force does

On October 4, 2026, President Trump announced the Super Intelligence Force (SIF) in a Truth Social post. It is a federal task force chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, who took the DNI role in August 2026 after serving as US attorney for the Southern District of New York and chairing the SEC during the first Trump term. Three vice chairs sit beside him: FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael, and OPM Director Scott Kupor.

  • Its job: the task force has 120 days to report on AI risks and opportunities and the federal government's role, coordinating with consumers, public interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers, and AI companies.
  • When the report is due: 120 days from October 4, 2026 lands around February 1, 2027; from September 29, around January 27, 2027. The start date is not settled in public reporting, so treat the deadline as approximate.
  • What it is not: the task force has no reported budget or statutory authority of its own. Its members keep their existing jobs, and its influence flows through the agencies they run.
  • First meeting: scheduled for October 15, 2026, at 4 p.m., with an invitation roster reaching across government: the vice president, the Treasury, Defense, and Commerce secretaries, the chief of staff, the OSTP director, and the national cyber director. Leadership said goals and responsibilities would be published afterward.
  • Early moves: reporting said Clayton would tour Silicon Valley AI firms for meetings with the heads of Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta.
  • What to watch: the proposed SI definition due November 28, 2026; whether the report lands on time; and which recommendations become policy. This site's newsletter sends one email when the report drops.

Infrastructure

What is Stargate?

Stargate is the $500 billion joint venture led by OpenAI with SoftBank and Oracle, targeting 10 GW of US AI compute. It is a program, not a single building: multiple campuses across the country, with the flagship in Abilene, Texas.

  • The flagship: developed by Crusoe on the Lancium Clean Campus and leased to Oracle and OpenAI. Eight buildings planned on about 4 million square feet with 1.2 GW of total power capacity. Construction since July 2024; the first NVIDIA GB200 racks arrived earlier this year. Reporting describes plans for 450,000 GB200 GPUs at the site.
  • Operating now: the first two buildings, 980,000 square feet supporting 200MW+ of IT load, are operational and used by Oracle Cloud Infrastructure for OpenAI.
  • The workforce: Crusoe says about 7,000 electricians, construction workers, and skilled tradespeople come to the site every day. The second phase, six more buildings, is expected to complete in mid-2026.
  • The pipeline: additional Stargate sites are in development in Shackelford County and Milam County in Texas, Lordstown in Ohio, and Doña Ana County in New Mexico.

Infrastructure

What is Colossus?

Colossus is SpaceXAI's supercomputer cluster across Memphis, Tennessee and Southaven, Mississippi, built to train the Grok model family and now leased to outside customers as well. It targets about 2 GW of total compute across the Memphis area.

  • Colossus 1: a former Electrolux plant in South Memphis. Roughly 100,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs went live in mid-to-late 2024 after a 122-day build, later joined by H200 and early B200 systems. In May 2026, Anthropic agreed to rent essentially all of its compute capacity.
  • Colossus 2 (Macrohard): at 5420 Tulane Road in Whitehaven. The first major cluster, about 110,000 GB200-class processors and roughly 210 MW, came online around January 2026 after a reported 91-day build. By late September 2026, 440,000 GB300 GPUs were in use with 660,000 more expected by the end of 2026.
  • Customers: Google agreed in June 2026 to rent about 110,000 GPUs for $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029.
  • Power: behind-the-meter gas turbines in Southaven filled the gap while permanent generation was built; a roughly 1.2 GW plant was permitted in March 2026, with temporary units scheduled for removal by mid-2027.

Infrastructure

Why do data centers talk in gigawatts?

Because electricity is the binding constraint on AI infrastructure. Training a frontier model means running hundreds of thousands of processors at full load for weeks or months, drawing enormous, steady power. Projects are sized by their power ceiling the way older data centers were sized by rack space.

  • The scale on this site: disclosed planned capacities run from about 1 GW per campus (Abilene, El Paso) to 2+ GW (Project Rainier, Colossus target) to 5 GW (Hyperion). A gigawatt is a billion watts, roughly the output of a large power station.
  • Why new power plants get built: the grid connection is often the bottleneck. Colossus ran temporary gas turbines while permanent generation was built in Southaven; Hyperion's power is planned from new Entergy plants targeting late 2028. The campus and its power supply are planned together.
  • Why it matters for the SI race: whoever can deliver gigawatts of reliable power, and the construction workforce to build for it, sets the pace of model training. The buildout table is, underneath, a map of where America is adding power for AI.

Hardware

What is a chip fab, and what are HBM and advanced packaging?

A fab is a factory that prints chips onto silicon wafers in cleanrooms far cleaner than a hospital operating room. The most advanced fabs etch features measured in nanometers: TSMC's Arizona Fab 21 Phase 1 runs 4nm, Phase 2 is built for 3nm, and Phase 3 under construction targets 2nm. A single fab can exceed a million square feet of cleanroom space and take years from groundbreaking to first wafers.

  • High bandwidth memory (HBM): memory stacked directly next to AI processors so data moves fast enough to keep them fed. Every AI accelerator pairs logic with HBM. SK hynix is building America's first HBM production hub, a $4B+ advanced packaging facility in West Lafayette, Indiana, targeting HBM4E volume production in the third quarter of 2029.
  • Advanced packaging: the assembly step that joins logic chips and memory into a working AI processor, including CoWoS (Chip on Wafer on Substrate). It is the industry's worst supply bottleneck, which is why TSMC plans a CoWoS packaging facility in Arizona targeted for 2029.
  • Why fabs take so long: they are among the most technically demanding construction projects in the world: ultra-high-purity water, specialized gases, seismic isolation, vast electrical infrastructure. Intel's Ohio campus has logged over 6.4 million labor hours and still targets first production in 2030-2031.

Questions

Explainer FAQ

Did the AI tools I use change because of the executive order?

No. The order changes the words US federal agencies use in their own documents. It defines SI using the existing legal definition of AI, so services like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude work exactly as they did before. No new capability was announced and no terms of service changed.

Is the government's SI the same as superintelligence in AI research?

No. In research, superintelligence means a hypothetical system that greatly exceeds human cognitive performance in virtually every domain. In the executive order, SI is a new label for everything already covered by the US statutory definition of AI. Always ask who wrote it and in what context.

When will there be a legal definition of Super Intelligence?

The order gives the president's science adviser 60 days, a deadline analysts date to November 28, 2026, to send the president proposed legislative language. That is only a proposal: replacing the statutory definition would need an Act of Congress.

What is the difference between Stargate and Colossus?

Stargate is OpenAI's joint venture with SoftBank and Oracle: a $500 billion program targeting 10 GW of US AI compute across multiple campuses. Colossus is SpaceXAI's cluster in Memphis and Southaven, targeting about 2 GW. Stargate is a multi-company program still being built out; Colossus is an operating cluster that is expanding.

Why do new data centers need their own power plants?

AI training clusters draw enormous, steady power, and the grid connection is often the constraint. Colossus used temporary gas turbines while permanent generation was built, and Hyperion's power is planned from new Entergy plants. The campuses are sized in gigawatts because that is the scale of their electricity demand.
