US chip fabs

New semiconductor production capacity on American soil: logic fabs, memory fabs, and the advanced packaging plants where AI chips are actually assembled. TSMC

Phoenix, Arizona

Announced investment: $265B total committed (expanded from $165B, July 2026)

Status: First fab in high-volume production (4nm/5nm); 12 fabs plus packaging and R&D planned

TSMC's Arizona commitment grew from an original $165 billion pledge to $265 billion in July 2026, when the company and the US Commerce Department confirmed an additional $100 billion, cementing the largest foreign direct investment in US history. The plan now covers 12 fabrication and packaging facilities plus an R&D center on more than 2,000 acres in north Phoenix, near Loop 303 and Interstate 17. Fab 21 Phase 1 began 4nm production in the first half of 2025 with US CHIPS Act support, is fully operational, and produces chips for Apple and NVIDIA, including NVIDIA's Blackwell AI processors, with yields described as comparable to TSMC's Taiwan facilities. Fab 21 Phase 2, built to 3nm specifications, completed construction ahead of schedule and entered pilot production about six months early, with high-volume 3nm production expected in 2027. A third fab at 2nm is under construction, and a first advanced packaging facility is targeted to open in 2029 with CoWoS (Chip on Wafer on Substrate) and 3D-IC integration, the advanced packaging technology that has become the critical bottleneck for AI chip production. At least four of the newly announced Arizona fabs are slated for 2nm-and-below process nodes.

Why it matters

Why it matters: TSMC Arizona is the only US site producing cutting-edge AI silicon at volume today. The 2029 packaging facility matters as much as the fabs: bringing CoWoS to Arizona would give customers a complete AI chip supply chain on American soil for the first time.

Sources: Tech Insider

Intel

New Albany, Ohio

Announced investment: Up to $28B planned (up to $100B potential long-term)

Status: Under construction at reduced pace; first production targeted 2030-2031

Intel's Ohio One campus in New Albany is an approximately $28 billion development, the company's flagship US foundry bet. The timeline has shifted: the first fab, once targeted for completion by 2025, is now expected to be completed in 2030 with operations beginning in 2030-2031, and the second fab is targeted for completion in 2031 with startup in 2032. Intel says the revised schedule aligns capital investment with market conditions and demand forecasts. Construction continues at a measured pace: the company has logged over 6.4 million labor hours and poured more than 200,000 cubic yards of concrete, with workforce training programs running at its other US sites. Intel has secured up to $7.865 billion in CHIPS Act funding, at least $1.5 billion of it designated for the Ohio campus. The slowdown coincides with broader restructuring under CEO Lip-Bu Tan, who has canceled projects in Germany, Poland, and Costa Rica.

Why it matters

Why it matters: Ohio One is the test case for whether a US-headquartered company can still build leading-edge logic at home. The delays show the other side of the boom: fabs are so capital-intensive that spending has to track confirmed demand, or the economics break.

Sources: Construction Review Online, Columbus Dispatch

Samsung

Taylor, Texas

Announced investment: $37B Texas investment (two logic fabs plus R&D)

Status: Fab 1 equipment move-in complete; risk production H2 2026; mass production targeted 2027; Fab 2 in planning

Samsung's Taylor, Texas campus is a $37 billion Texas investment covering two logic fabs plus R&D on a 1,268-acre site, the largest foreign investment in Texas history. Construction resumed at full scale after a mid-2024 pause. Fab 1 has completed equipment move-in, is targeting risk production in the second half of 2026, and mass production in 2027. Fab 2, projected at about 2.7 million square feet, is in planning and regulatory review. Samsung says the campus is expected to create 1,800 permanent jobs within a decade, and its own reporting says the project supported 8,868 direct and 9,768 indirect construction jobs in 2024.

Why it matters

Why it matters: Taylor is the fab most likely to add major US logic capacity next after TSMC Arizona. Its construction job numbers, nearly 18,600 direct and indirect in 2024, show what a single fab means for a local labor market.

Sources: Tech Insider, Tech Times, BuildWCG

Micron

Clay, New York and Boise, Idaho

Announced investment: $100B megafab in Clay, NY; $30B Boise, ID expansion; $200B total US program

Status: Clay megafab broke ground Jan 2026, first production around 2030; Boise expansion targeting first wafers H2 2027

Micron broke ground in January 2026 on its $100 billion megafab in Clay, New York, which Governor Kathy Hochul called the largest private investment in New York history. The Clay facility will produce DRAM memory chips across multiple phases, with first production expected around 2030. It anchors a $200 billion total US program: $150 billion in fabrication plants and $50 billion in research and development across Idaho, New York, and Virginia. When complete, the program is projected to supply 40% of Micron's global DRAM output from US facilities and generate an estimated 90,000 direct and indirect jobs. Separately, Micron is expanding its Boise, Idaho campus with a $30 billion expansion aided by a CHIPS Act grant, targeting first wafer output in the second half of 2027.

Why it matters

Why it matters: memory is the other half of the AI chip stack. Every AI accelerator needs high-bandwidth memory next to its logic, and Micron's program would put 40% of its global DRAM output on US soil.

Sources: BuildWCG

SK hynix

West Lafayette, Indiana

Announced investment: Over $4B

Status: Advanced HBM packaging facility; groundbreaking Aug 27, 2026; cleanroom target Oct 2028, production H2 2029

SK hynix is building an advanced packaging facility for high bandwidth memory at Purdue Research Park in West Lafayette, Indiana: over $4 billion, the company's first US HBM production hub. Piling and foundation work began in April 2026, and the ceremonial groundbreaking on August 27, 2026 drew Indiana Governor Mike Braun and Senator Todd Young. The project carries up to $458 million in CHIPS Act grants and up to $570 million in federal loans. The cleanroom is targeted for October 2028, with HBM4E volume production expected in the third quarter of 2029. The facility is expected to support around 1,000 facility jobs, and Purdue University and SK hynix formalized a research agreement spanning advanced packaging and memory technologies.

Why it matters

Why it matters: this is packaging, not wafer fabrication, and that is exactly the point. Advanced packaging is where AI chips are assembled and where the industry's worst bottleneck sits. A US HBM hub at a research university ties manufacturing to the talent pipeline directly.

Sources: Construction Review Online, Aju Press

Questions

Chip fabs FAQ

Answers grounded in the sourced profiles above.

Which fab is producing chips in the US right now?

TSMC's Fab 21 Phase 1 in Phoenix, Arizona: 4nm chips in high-volume production since early 2025, supplying Apple and NVIDIA. Samsung's Taylor, Texas Fab 1 is targeting mass production in 2027. The other tracked projects are under construction or in planning.

What are HBM and advanced packaging, and why do they matter for AI?

High bandwidth memory (HBM) is memory stacked directly next to AI processors so data moves fast enough to keep them fed. Advanced packaging is the assembly step that joins logic chips and memory, including CoWoS (Chip on Wafer on Substrate). Both are bottlenecks for AI chip supply, which is why SK hynix is building a $4B+ HBM packaging hub in Indiana and TSMC plans a CoWoS packaging facility in Arizona targeted for 2029.

How much federal money is behind these fabs?

Reported CHIPS and Science Act support includes up to $7.865 billion for Intel (at least $1.5 billion for Ohio), a CHIPS Act grant aiding Micron's $30 billion Boise expansion, and up to $458 million in grants plus up to $570 million in federal loans for SK hynix in Indiana. TSMC's Arizona Fab 21 Phase 1 received CHIPS Act support. These are reported awards, not the full project costs.

Why do fab timelines slip so much?

Fabs cost tens of billions of dollars and take years from groundbreaking to first wafers. Intel's Ohio campus shows the mechanism: the company slowed construction to align capital spending with market demand, pushing first operations from a 2025 target to 2030-2031. Announced timelines are plans; only completed construction is capacity.

What does risk production mean?

Risk production is the trial manufacturing phase before mass production: a fab runs real wafers through the line to prove out the process while yields are still being tuned. Samsung's Taylor Fab 1 is targeting risk production in the second half of 2026 ahead of mass production in 2027.
