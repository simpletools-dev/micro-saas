Common heating problems

  • Furnace won't ignite — failed ignitor or flame sensor is the usual suspect; both are routine fixes.
  • Blowing cold air — could be a thermostat setting, a tripped limit switch from overheating (often a clogged filter), or a gas supply issue.
  • Short cycling — system fires then shuts off repeatedly. Overheating, a bad flame sensor, or an oversized unit.
  • Strange smells on first fall startup — dusty burners smell for a few minutes; a persistent gas or electrical smell means shut it down and call.
  • Heat pump stuck in one mode — reversing valve or defrost-control issues; heat pumps are less common here but we service them.
  • Rising gas bills with no change in use — an aging furnace losing efficiency, or duct leaks dumping heat into the attic.
Carbon monoxide warning: gas furnaces produce CO when combustion goes wrong — cracked heat exchangers are the classic cause. Symptoms (headaches, nausea, dizziness that ease outdoors) mean: get out, call the gas utility, then have the furnace inspected before using it again. We check heat exchangers on every furnace call.

Fall checklist before heating season

  • Replace the furnace filter (then keep doing it every 1–3 months).
  • Make sure supply registers and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture.
  • Test the thermostat in HEAT mode before the first cold night.
  • Check that CO detectors have fresh batteries — one on each floor, near bedrooms.
  • Schedule a furnace safety inspection once a year.

Questions to ask your technician

  • Did you inspect the heat exchanger for cracks?
  • Did you test for carbon monoxide at the supply registers?
  • Is this repair worth it on a furnace this age, or am I buying time?
  • What's the efficiency (AFUE) of my current furnace vs. a new one?

Common questions

How long do furnaces last in Bakersfield?

15–20 years is common — our mild winters mean less runtime than in cold climates. But age plus a cracked heat exchanger means replacement, not repair.

Why does my heater smell when I first turn it on?

Dust burning off the heat exchanger — normal for a few minutes each fall. If it persists, or smells like gas or burning plastic, shut it off and call.

Do I really need a CO detector?

Yes — California requires them, and they're the only way to catch a furnace CO problem before symptoms. Test them twice a year.
