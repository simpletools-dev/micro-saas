Common AC failures we fix

  • Bad capacitor — the #1 summer repair. The fan or compressor hums but won't start. Quick, affordable fix — we carry them on the truck.
  • Low refrigerant — ice on the lines, weak cooling. There's always a leak causing it; we find and fix the leak rather than just topping off.
  • Dirty condenser coil — Kern County dust chokes outdoor coils. Cleaning restores cooling you didn't know you'd lost.
  • Failed contactor or relay — the unit won't turn on at all, or chatters when starting.
  • Frozen evaporator coil — caused by low airflow (dirty filter) or low refrigerant. Turn the system off and let it thaw before we arrive.
  • Blower motor failure — air handler runs weak or not at all; some rooms get nothing.
  • Thermostat issues — miscalibrated or dead thermostats mimic bigger problems. We check this first.

What to check before you call

  • Is the thermostat set to COOL and the temperature set below room temp?
  • Is the air filter clogged? (A black filter can freeze the coil.)
  • Is the breaker tripped?
  • Is the outdoor unit blocked by weeds, trash, or stored items? (Give it 2 feet of clearance.)

If those check out and it's still not cooling, call — running a struggling system can turn a small repair into a compressor replacement.

Repair vs. replace

Repair when the system is under ~12 years old and the fix is a component (capacitor, contactor, fan motor, even a coil). Replace when the compressor fails on an older unit, the system uses phased-out refrigerant and leaks, or you're stacking repairs year after year. The 50% rule is a decent guide: if the repair costs more than half the value of a comparable new system and yours is old, replace.

Questions to ask your technician

  • What exactly failed, and what caused it?
  • Is this repair a lasting fix or a band-aid on a dying system?
  • What's the honest cost comparison: this repair vs. replacement?
  • Did you check refrigerant charge and airflow, not just swap the part?

Common questions

Why is my AC running but not cooling?

Most often: dirty filter or coil, low refrigerant from a leak, or a failing compressor. Check the filter and breaker first, then call — don't let it run for days struggling.

How long should an AC last in Bakersfield?

12–15 years is typical here with annual maintenance. Our long, brutal cooling season ages systems faster than in mild climates.

Should I cover my outdoor unit in winter?

No — covers trap moisture and invite rodents. Just keep debris cleared and get a spring tune-up.
