What we clear

Kitchen drains

Grease is the #1 kitchen clog. It coats pipes over years until water barely passes. We cut through it mechanically — no chemical guesswork.

Bathroom drains

Hair and soap scum in tubs, showers, and sinks. Usually a quick auger job once we get eyes on it.

Main sewer lines

Multiple fixtures backing up at once means the main line. Tree roots love older clay and cast-iron sewer lines in established neighborhoods.

Laundry & floor drains

Lint buildup and sediment in laundry standpipes and garage/floor drains.

Snaking vs. hydro jetting

Drain auger (snake)

A rotating cable that bores through the blockage. Right tool for most single-fixture clogs — fast and affordable. It clears the clog but doesn't fully clean the pipe walls.

Hydro jetting

High-pressure water that scours the entire pipe interior — the real fix for grease-choked kitchen lines and root-intruded sewer lines. Costs more than snaking but lasts far longer on the right jobs.

Signs of a main-line problem

  • Toilets gurgling when the washer drains, or tubs filling when toilets flush
  • Sewage smell from drains or in the yard
  • Multiple slow drains at the same time
  • Water backing up in the lowest drain in the house

Older Oildale, Greenfield, and East Bakersfield homes often have original clay or cast-iron sewer laterals — prime territory for root intrusion. A camera inspection shows exactly what's going on down there before anyone digs.

What not to put down drains

  • Grease and cooking oil — wipe pans out and trash it, even with a garbage disposal.
  • "Flushable" wipes — they don't break down like toilet paper and are a top cause of main-line clogs.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells — they settle and pack in the line.
  • Chemical drain cleaners — they can damage older pipes and make a plumber's job hazardous.

Common questions

How much does drain cleaning cost?

A simple single-fixture auger is the low end; main-line work with camera inspection and jetting costs more. We quote on site after seeing the problem — you'll approve the price first.

Why does my drain keep clogging in the same spot?

Because the underlying cause — grease buildup, roots, a bellied pipe section — was never addressed. A camera inspection finds the real problem so it stops coming back.

How often should sewer lines be cleaned?

If you've had root problems, every 1–2 years of preventive jetting beats emergency backups. Otherwise, only when symptoms appear.
