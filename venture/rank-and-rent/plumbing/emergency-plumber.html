Plumbing emergencies we handle

  • Burst or leaking pipes — shut the main off first, then call. We'll walk you through finding it on the phone if you don't know where it is.
  • Sewage backup — stop using water immediately and keep people away from the affected area. This is a health hazard, not a wait-until-morning situation.
  • Major drain overflow — water coming up through tubs or floor drains usually means a main-line blockage.
  • Water heater leaking or ruptured — shut the water and the gas/electric to the unit, then call.
  • No water to the house — if neighbors have water, the problem is on your side of the meter.
  • Gas smell near a water heater or furnace — leave the house and call the gas utility first, then a plumber once it's safe.

What to do in the first 5 minutes

  1. Shut off the water. Main shutoff (curb box or where the line enters the house) for a burst pipe; the fixture valve under the sink/toilet for a localized leak.
  2. Kill power to wet areas if water is near outlets or the breaker panel — flip the breaker if you can do it safely and dry.
  3. Contain what you can. Towels, buckets, and moving valuables off the floor. Don't wade into standing water near electrical.
  4. Call us. Describe what you see and what you've shut off — it helps the tech bring the right parts.
For sewage backups: avoid contact with the water, don't run dishwashers or laundry, and keep kids and pets away. We'll prioritize these calls.

Common questions

How fast can an emergency plumber get here?

It depends on time of day and truck locations, but emergency calls jump the queue. We'll give you an honest arrival window on the phone — not a vague 'sometime today.'

Is there an extra fee for after-hours calls?

Emergency and after-hours dispatch can include a service fee. We'll state it clearly on the phone before sending anyone — you approve it first.

Should I try to fix a burst pipe myself?

Shutting off the water yourself: yes, absolutely. Repairing the pipe: a temporary clamp or tape can slow a pinhole leak, but proper repair needs a pro — especially on pressurized lines.
