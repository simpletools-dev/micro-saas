Opener types we service

Chain drive

Affordable and strong — the most common opener in Bakersfield homes. Louder than belt drives, which matters if a bedroom sits over the garage.

Belt drive

Quietest option thanks to the rubber belt. Ideal for attached garages under living space. Slightly higher cost, worth it for the noise difference.

Screw drive

Fewer moving parts and good for wide, heavy doors. Needs occasional lubrication in dusty conditions.

Jackshaft (wall-mount)

Mounts beside the door instead of overhead — frees up ceiling space and runs very quietly. Great for garages with high or obstructed ceilings.

Common opener problems

  • Motor runs, door doesn't move — usually a stripped drive gear, a very common and inexpensive fix.
  • Door reverses right before closing — almost always misaligned or dirty safety sensors at the bottom of the track.
  • Remote or keypad is intermittent — dead batteries first; then signal interference or a failing receiver/logic board.
  • Opener hums but strains — often a door-balance problem (springs) rather than the opener itself. We check both.
  • Very old opener with no safety sensors — pre-1993 units lack auto-reverse. Replacement isn't just convenient, it's a safety upgrade.

Repair vs. replace

Repair when the opener is under ~10 years old and the issue is a gear, sensor, remote, or capacitor. Replace when the motor itself is failing, parts are discontinued, or the unit lacks modern safety features. A new belt-drive opener with battery backup (required on new installs in California) typically runs a few hundred dollars installed — we'll quote both options so you can decide.

Questions to ask your technician

  • Is the problem the opener, or the door balance/springs?
  • What's the cost difference between this repair and a new opener?
  • Does the replacement include battery backup and new safety sensors?
  • Will my existing remotes and keypad work with the new unit?

Common questions

Why does my garage door only open a few inches then stop?

Usually a broken spring making the door too heavy for the opener, or a force-setting issue. Don't keep trying it — you can burn out the opener motor.

How long do garage door openers last?

Typically 10–15 years. Chain drives often outlast their electronics; when the logic board or motor goes on an older unit, replacement usually beats repair.

Do I need Wi-Fi on my opener?

Not essential, but it's genuinely useful — phone alerts if the door is left open, and remote operation from anywhere. Most new models include it.
