Choosing the right door

Steel doors

The workhorse choice for Bakersfield homes: affordable, low-maintenance, and available insulated. Look for a polyurethane-insulated model (R-value 12+) if your garage shares a wall with living space — it noticeably cuts heat transfer during 100°+ summer days.

Wood & carriage-house style

Real wood or wood-look composite doors suit older neighborhoods like Westchester and Oleander. They cost more and need periodic refinishing in our dry, sunny climate, but the curb appeal is unmatched.

Sizing basics

  • Single-car: usually 8 or 9 ft wide, 7 or 8 ft tall
  • Double-car: usually 16 ft wide — the most common replacement we install
  • Measure twice: we confirm the rough opening, headroom, and side room on site before ordering

Insulation matters here

Bakersfield summers regularly top 105°F. An insulated door keeps the garage 15–25° cooler than a non-insulated one, which matters if you use the garage as a workshop, gym, or laundry space.

What drives the price

  • Door size and material — a basic non-insulated steel single door is the budget end; insulated wood-look doubles are the premium end.
  • Insulation level — higher R-value costs more upfront, pays back in comfort.
  • Hardware and track — heavy-duty tracks and quiet nylon rollers add modest cost and a lot of daily satisfaction.
  • Opener pairing — many homeowners replace the opener at the same time; ask about bundle pricing.
  • Removal and haul-away — included in our quotes, but always confirm it's in writing with any contractor.
Tip: get the quote in writing with the exact door model, insulation R-value, and warranty terms. A vague "new garage door" line item is where misunderstandings start.

Questions to ask any installer

  • What is the exact door model and insulation R-value?
  • Is removal and haul-away of the old door included?
  • What are the warranties on the door, the springs, and the labor — and who honors each one?
  • Will you re-use my existing tracks, or install new ones? (New tracks are usually worth it.)
  • How long will the install take, and is the opener included in the price?

Common questions

How long does installation take?

A standard replacement takes about 3–5 hours, including removing the old door. Custom or oversized doors can take longer.

Can I keep my existing opener?

Often yes, if it's in good shape and has modern safety sensors. We'll test it during the estimate and tell you honestly whether it's worth keeping.

Do new doors meet California safety requirements?

Yes — we install doors with pinch-resistant panels and auto-reverse safety systems that meet current standards.
