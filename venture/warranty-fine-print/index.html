Home Warranty Fine Print, Translated

Home warranty companies advertise coverage. What actually decides your payout is the fine print: per-item caps, exclusions, waiting periods, and cancellation terms. We read the sample contracts and third-party reviews so you can compare the numbers that matter.

The 5-minute rule: before buying any home warranty, find these five numbers in the sample contract: (1) per-item coverage cap for HVAC, (2) per-item cap for appliances, (3) the service call fee, (4) the waiting period, and (5) what counts as a pre-existing condition. If a company won't show you the sample contract, walk away.

What home warranties actually cover

  • Systems: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, water heater, ductwork, garage door opener.
  • Appliances: refrigerator, oven/range, dishwasher, built-in microwave, washer/dryer, garbage disposal.
  • How they pay: repair first, replace only if repair is impossible - and only up to the per-item cap. You pay the service call fee on every claim, plus anything above the cap.
  • What's usually excluded: known pre-existing conditions, routine maintenance, misuse, cosmetic damage, code upgrades, and items outside the main home foundation.

How to read the fine print (the traps)

  1. Caps beat premiums. A $45/mo plan with a $500 appliance cap is worse than a $60/mo plan with a $2,000 cap if your fridge dies.
  2. "Unknown" pre-existing conditions. Only some companies cover breakdowns they didn't know about. The rest deny anything that predates your start date.
  3. Waiting periods. Standard is 30 days. Buying the day your AC dies does not work.
  4. Service fee math. A $150 fee on a cheap plan can cost more per year than a $100 fee on a pricier plan if you file two claims.
  5. Cancellation terms. Most give 30 days for a refund; after that, refunds are pro-rated and reduced by claims paid and admin fees.
  6. Workmanship guarantees range from 30 days to a full year - this is how long a botched repair stays the company's problem.
