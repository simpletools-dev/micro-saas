What the AI to SI rename means for you

Covers events from September 22 to October 6, 2026

On September 29, 2026, President Trump signed Executive Order 14434, titled "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence." The order directs executive branch agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in official non-statutory communications, and tasks the science adviser with proposing a formal federal definition of the term.

What that means in practice:

  • Federal documents will say "super intelligence" or "SI." Expect the new wording in agency press releases, reports, and other official communications.
  • It does not rewrite laws or regulations by itself. The order covers non-statutory communications. Statutes that say "artificial intelligence" still say that until Congress changes them.
  • Private companies choose their own wording. The order binds the executive branch, not businesses or the press, though some companies have adopted the new term voluntarily.

Related moves around the same time: on September 22 the rename was floated publicly at the UN General Assembly; on September 29 the chief executives of Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI, and Nvidia signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, a voluntary industry commitment on AI safety controls and incident reporting; and on October 6 the Justice Department issued a staff memo implementing the new terminology in official communications and, where appropriate, court filings.

One visible side effect: the rename drove a surge in .si domain registrations, with 44,000 new registrations in September 2026 versus under 2,000 in August, according to Slovenia's domain registry as reported in early October.

What the Super Intelligence Force does

Announced October 4, 2026; first meeting October 15, 2026

The Super Intelligence Force (SIF) is a federal task force announced by President Trump in a Truth Social post on October 4, 2026. It is chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, with three vice chairs.

Its assignment: within 120 days, report on AI risks and opportunities and on the federal government's role. A 120-day clock from October 4 implies a report around early February 2027, though that date is approximate and the task force itself has not published a confirmed deadline.

The task force held its first meeting on October 15, 2026, with leadership expected to publish its goals and responsibilities afterward.

What it is and is not: the SIF is an announced executive-branch task force, not legislation. Its output will be a report with findings and recommendations; any policy changes would come through separate executive or legislative action. As of October 16, 2026, the report has not been published.

Explainer 3

How the SI readiness tiers work

Methodology, October 2026

The SI readiness tiers are our editorial way of summarizing where announced AI data center and chip fab buildout stands in each state. They are built from one thing only: verified announced projects, each cited to its source on the relevant state page.

How tiers were assigned

  1. We collected announced AI data center projects (71) and chip fab projects (17) across the 50 states, each with a cited source.
  2. For each state we counted its projects and weighed their reported scale and status: operational, under construction, or announced.
  3. States with the largest concentration of major announcements became Leaders (15). States with a smaller but real footprint became Builders (20). States with one or two early projects became Emerging (8). States where we found no major announced projects became Watching (7).

What each tier means

  • Leader States with the largest concentration of announced AI data center and chip fab projects: multiple major campuses, headline multi-billion dollar commitments, or both.
  • Builder States with a meaningful announced footprint: at least one significant campus or fab, ahead of most of the country but below the largest hubs.
  • Emerging States showing early signs of buildout: one or two announced projects, with the footprint still small next to the leading states.
  • Watching States where our research found no major announced AI data center or chip fab projects as of October 2026.

Honest limits

  • Announced is not built. Projects stall, shrink, or get canceled. A tier reflects announcements as of October 2026, not completed construction.
  • Figures come from companies and press. Investment numbers and job projections are reported claims, cited per project, not independently audited by us.
  • Coverage is finite. We tracked major announced projects; smaller facilities and unannounced projects are outside the count.
  • Tiers can move. A single large announcement can lift a state. We update the data when the project landscape changes.
